## What differs from your proposal

Six things. Each is on the contract page; this is the list so you do not have to diff it.

1. **A ninth kind, `delivery_fee_refund`** (your D-3). Hand-settled delivery refunds write no
   `refund_transactions` row. Without the kind, money returned to customers by hand would be
   missing from "all transactions".
2. **`direction` is fixed per kind and never follows the status** (your D-6). A failed payment
   is `in` with `outcome: failed`. The account feed's "a pending payout is `internal`" rule does
   not carry over, because it is the owner's reading of the owner's balances. Here "pay-ins that
   failed" is two filters, and no per-status table exists to get wrong.
3. **`outcome` has a fifth value, `unknown`.** A status jovi-mall adds later is shown as
   `unknown` and is findable by that word. It is never guessed into `pending`.
4. **`amountGt` / `amountLt` exist.** You asked us to confirm whole-number amounts or give
   exclusive bounds. We cannot confirm it — nothing forces a stored amount to be an integer — so
   the bounds are there. Do not send `n ± 1`.
5. **A filter on a column a kind lacks excludes that kind.** `?provider=MTN` returns no refund,
   cash or ledger row. You did not ask for the opposite, but it is the rule most likely to
   surprise: the result is narrower than "ignore the term where it does not apply".
6. **Totals are on `/summary`, not on `meta`** (your D-8), and they are a group-by, not a net:
   one cell per kind, direction, outcome and currency. The page says which roll-ups are safe.

Two smaller ones: `party.role` can read `platform_ai` (the bargain-fee account; `role=platform`
matches both), and a refund's `method` is its channel.

## § C — why a cursor, and what it guarantees

You listed three requirements in order. All three hold.

| Requirement | How |
|---|---|
| Every filter in the database | Each of the nine sources gets its own native filter. Derived columns (`outcome`, `direction`, `role`, `reasonCode`) translate to predicates on stored fields. The handler filters nothing afterwards, and a test scans it for that |
| A stable order, nothing repeated or skipped | A **keyset** on `(sort value, id)`. The same "everything after this point" question goes to all nine collections, so the merge of their answers is exact. Rows sharing a millisecond or an amount are ordered by id |
| `total` wanted, not required | Not on the list. `/summary` has `count`, asked once rather than on every page turn |

A page is exactly `limit` rows, unlike the account feed's, which can run long. `cursor` is
opaque and is refused under a different `sort`.

**The date window is always applied.** With no `from` and no `to` you get the last 366 days, and
`meta.window` says so. This is the one place the list can look shorter than "everything":
please show the window on the screen.

Indexes were added in jovi-mall for the date walk (eight collections). The list is correct
without them; it is just a scan.

## § D — the detail read

Between your composed read and your smaller form.

- **`related.transactions`** is the list itself, filtered to the row's order(s) or booking and
  graded for the caller. So a payment opens with its refunds, top-ups, delivery-fee refunds and
  (tiers 1–2) the earnings movements it caused — without a hand-built composition per kind.
- **`recordPath`** points at the full read you already render, for the five kinds that have one.
  We did not compose a second copy of those five.
- **`record`** carries the extra facts for the four kinds that have none: refund (fee kept, net
  sent, who asked, vendor), plan purchase, credit top-up, ledger entry (balances after, and its
  allocation in full).

What your § D table lists and this does **not** return inline: a payout's ticket and
endorsement (on `recordPath`), a deposit's proof and cash movements (on `recordPath`), the
refund request and the subscription (ids in `links` and `record`; open them where they live).

## § E — access

Exactly your proposal, with your one open question decided.

- **No `money.transactions.read`.** A permission granted to every tier would have been a second
  way to reach rows the first way withholds. The route opens on any one of the five existing
  reads, and each kind is sent only to a caller holding that kind's own reads. The table is on
  the contract page; Support gets six kinds.
- **Platform ledger rows: tier 1 only**, excluded in the query for every filter. A tier-2 caller
  sending `?role=platform&kind=ledger_entry` gets an empty page.
- **`?kind=ledger_entry` for a caller who may not see it → an empty page with `meta.withheld`**,
  not a `403`. One rule covers the single-kind and the mixed filter.
- **The detail read is the opposite**: a kind you may not read is `403` naming the permission; a
  platform ledger row below tier 1 is `404`.

⚠ **One thing this does not change.** `GET /money/earnings/platform`, `…/platform/ledger` and
`…/platform/summary` still answer tier 2, and `GET /money/orders/:orderId/split` still shows
every tier the platform's line. The owner's rule you quoted is enforced on the new list only.
Whether to enforce it on those four existing routes is a question for the owner, below.

## Two decisions that are the owner's, not ours

Both change what "everything" means, so neither was decided here.

**1 · Is a customer's cash-on-delivery payment a row?**
- Today: it is not. A COD order has no pay-in row; its cash first appears on the agent's deposit,
  which usually covers several orders.
- If yes: a tenth kind (`cod_collection`), one row per delivery paid in cash, findable by order.
  It needs a ruling on direction, or the same cash is counted at collection and again at
  remittance.
- If no: the list stays as built, and the page should say that COD customer payments are not on it.
- Our recommendation: **yes**, as `internal` (the cash is in the agent's hands, not the
  platform's), so `in` still means money the platform holds.

**2 · Are refund-debt write-offs and payout-driven debt recoveries rows?**
- Today: they are not. They write no ledger row; their record is `earnings_adjustments`.
- If yes: an eleventh kind, limited to the adjustments with no ledger entry, so nothing repeats.
- If no: a forgiven debt is visible on the clawback screen and in the audit log only.
- Our recommendation: **yes**, but it can follow; the amounts are rare.

**3 · Should the four existing platform-earnings routes follow the tier-1 rule too?** See § E.
