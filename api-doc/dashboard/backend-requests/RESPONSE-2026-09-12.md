## Why not (a), and why not (b)

**Your objection to (a) was the right one, and it is stronger than you put it.** It is not a
judgement call that charging the party is bad — it contradicts that counter's *stated purpose*. The
constants' own docblock says the per-party limit is *"a harassment and SMS-bill bound (the party
did not ask for any of these)"*. A channel the party does not have sends them nothing. Charging
them for it is not a trade-off; it is the counter measuring something it was not built to measure.

**But the same docblock rules out (b).** The per-administrator limit *"bounds a compromised or
careless operator account"* — and an enumeration sweep is precisely a careless or compromised
operator account. Leaving the probe uncounted contradicts *that* counter's stated purpose just as
squarely. (b) would have meant writing down that we accept the oracle, when one of the two
instruments built to bound it was sitting unused.

**So the two counters wanted opposite orderings, which is why no single call site could be right.**
They are now spent in different places:

| Counter | Spent | A refused channel |
|---|---|---|
| **per administrator** (30/hr) | on the **attempt**, before the channel resolves | **costs** the caller their allowance |
| **per party** (3/hr) | once the channel **resolves** | costs the party **nothing** |

What this buys, in your terms: **the mis-click you were worried about is free for the party.** A
`409` on `email` does not consume one of that party's three, so the operator immediately tries
`whatsapp` and it works. And the sweep you were worried about now runs out — 30 an hour, the same
allowance a real send costs.

**The oracle is bounded, not closed, and we are saying so rather than implying otherwise.** Three
requests still answer the question for one party. What is gone is doing it across the platform for
free. Your framing was right: this is contact metadata and the caller already holds a permission to
send to that contact. The defect was never the oracle — it was a sentence claiming the oracle was
impossible.

# BR-022 — sharpened, and the strict set is six times larger than you measured

**You found 2 strict endpoints. There are 12.** Your instinct that *"a grep for `.strict()` on your

## How the set was actually derived

the behaviour a client actually meets. The grep would have been wrong twice:

- **It over-reports.** Two strict schemas were bound to **no route at all** —
  `DelegatedPageQuerySchema` and `ListNotesQuerySchema`. A grep counts them; a client can never

`quotaBlockedAt` is read in precisely two places that affect what you see, and **neither is a

`product-image.resolver.ts` (excludes blocked files from product galleries). We checked the whole

holding its URL. Enforcing in the content route would refuse the one caller who is authenticated,
permissioned and audited while leaving a stale anonymous link untouched. That is a good argument
and we would not have made it as well.

## A note on the four

These arrived together and three of them are the same bug: **a sentence stating a property the
code did not have.** Not a stale sentence — a *checkable* one, each giving a reason a reader should
trust it. The throttle one named an attacker. The quota one told you not to bother calling. The
strictness one generalised a true statement about a helper into a false statement about a service.

Each was read repeatedly on this side without being checked, and each took you under ten minutes to
disprove against a running server. The lesson we are taking is narrower than *"verify docs against
code"*: **a documented claim that names an attack, a guarantee, or a "will not work" is a claim
that should have a test**, because it is exactly the sort a client builds on. Both guards added this
round exist for that reason.

BR-023 is the one that cost something, and it cost it because the sentence was believed, implemented
six surfaces deep, and then tested by tests written from the same sentence. Neither the loss nor the
recovery is on you — you found it, reported it rather than working around it, and fixed all six the
same day. Keep sending them.
