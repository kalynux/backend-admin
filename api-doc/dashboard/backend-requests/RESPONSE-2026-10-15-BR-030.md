## § 1 · `cod_collection`

Everything in your table, with these answers to its open points:

- **`amount` is `expected_amount`**, the only amount the record has. A collection is all or
  nothing (the agent enters the customer's code and the row turns `collected`), so on a
  `collected` row it is what was collected, on a `pending` row what is due. The page says so.
- **Outcome** is your mapping: `collected` → `succeeded`, `pending` → `pending`,
  `cancelled` → `failed`.
- **`reason.code` is one per `kind`:** `cod_order_payment` (the goods, with any delivery fee) and
  `cod_delivery_fee` (a fee alone, on an order paid online). A row written before fee-only
  collections existed has no `kind` and reads as `cod_order_payment`.
- **The agent is a link.** `links` gained **`agentId`** — and **`payoutId`**, for § 2 — so every
  row now has 12 link keys, `null` where they do not apply. The agent's name is on `record.agent`.
- **`record`**: `collects`, `itemsAmount`, `deliveryFeeAmount`, `settledAmount`, `settledAt`,
  `agent { id, name }`. On a pre-split row `itemsAmount` is the amount less any delivery fee.
- **Access: `shipments.read`.** You named the order's `cod` block and the COD reads; the record is
  actually shown today on the **shipment detail** (`GET /shipments/:shipmentId`), so that is the
  read. Every tier holds it, so Support sees collections, as they already do on that screen.
- **Never returned:** `code_hash`, `code_plain`, `code_attempts`, `code_locked` and the whole
  `verification` block. The source does not read them. The live suite seeds all of them and checks
  none reach the list or the detail.

The **"one banknote, up to three rows, exactly one `in`"** explanation is on the contract page,
with a table, as you asked.

One more change you did not ask for: **`test:money` used to fail if the money module named
`cash_collections` at all.** That ban protected the delivery code, so it is now "exactly one
reader, and the delivery code and verification block named nowhere".

## § 2 · `earnings_adjustment`, and a correction to our own BR-027 reply

The BR-027 reply said these were *"the adjustments with no ledger entry"*. Reading jovi-mall's
writers, that is wrong for one case. A refund that charges a vendor **beyond their share** writes
an adjustment with no allocation, **and** a ledger row filed under the vendor's share on that sale
whenever there is one, which is the normal case. Listing every allocation-less adjustment would
have shown that charge twice.

So the kind is exactly the two movements you named:

| Kind | What | Ledger entry? |
|---|---|---|
| `earnings_adjustment`, reason `debt_write_off` | An administrator forgave the debt | none — this is its only row |
| `earnings_adjustment`, reason `debt_recovered_from_payout` | A rejected or failed payout came back and paid the debt down | none — this is its only row |
| `ledger_entry`, reason `refund_clawback` | A refund took money back from a share, or charged a vendor beyond it | yes |
| `ledger_entry`, reason `clawback_recovery` | A later release paid the debt down | yes |

The page has the sentence an operator can be told: *a write-off and a debt paid by a returned
payout are adjustments; everything else a refund does to someone's earnings is a ledger entry.*
`test:transactions` reads jovi-mall's writers and fails if that stops being true.

⚠ **One rare charge is on neither.** If a vendor is charged beyond their share and has **no share
at all** on that sale, nothing writes a ledger row. That charge is still on the order's money split
under `adjustments`. The page says so.

Your other points:

- **Access:** confirmed. `money.earnings.read` (tiers 1–2, not Support), and an adjustment owned
  by a platform account follows the platform rule like a ledger row does.
- **`links.payoutId`** is set on a payout recovery. It is parsed from jovi-mall's key
  (`recovery:payout:<id>:<event>`); `record.payoutEvent` is `rejected` or `ticket_failed`.
- **The refund request or order the debt came from: not available.** A debt is the owner's running
  balance. Neither movement records which refund created what it settles.
- **`record`:** `adjustment`, `actor { id, name }` (who forgave it; `null` on a recovery) and
  `payoutEvent`. **Whether a write-off went through four-eyes, and the debt before and after, are
  not stored on the record.** Both are in the audit log under
  `money.earnings.clawback.write_off_<vendor|agency|agent>`.

Also corrected: the page listed `clawback_write_off` as a ledger reason code. Nothing writes it.

## § 3b · The order split

Every response carries `platformAmountsWithheld` (`true` / `false`). Below the permission:

| Set to `null` | Kept |
|---|---|
| `amount`, `clawedAmount` on every `platform` / `platform_ai` line | every rate: `commission.percent`, `bargainFee.percent`, `bargainFeePercent` |
| `goods.commission.amount`, `goods.bargainFee.amount`, every bargained item's `fee` | `commission.base`, each item's price, floor and `uplift` |
| `totals.platform` | the vendor's, agencies' and agents' figures, `charged`, `reconciliation` |
| the amounts on an `adjustments` row against a platform account | every other adjustment |

Everything it touched also carries **`withheld: true`**, so the panel can say *"not shown at your
level"* rather than read the `null` as *"not known yet"*. Your boundary caveat is on the page,
worded as yours: the service stops stating the amounts, and they can still be worked out.

Not withheld, deliberately: `commission.base` (what the rate applies to) and each item's `uplift`.
Both explain the rate, and you asked for the rates to stay. Say if the owner wants those gone
too; it is one line each.
